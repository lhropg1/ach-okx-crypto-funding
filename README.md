## Is buying crypto with a bank transfer a good idea?

For many buyers, yes. Bank transfers are generally useful when you want to deposit more than a card provider would comfortably allow, avoid card processing charges, or make recurring purchases from a linked bank account.

OKX describes ACH as a U.S.-based electronic payment network that lets verified customers connect a bank account through Plaid and buy crypto directly from the trading interface. ACH purchases generally have no separate payment fee, although the final transaction preview should always be checked before confirmation.

The main advantages are:

- **Lower payment costs:** ACH purchases generally have no fee on OKX, while card payments often include a separate processing charge.
- **Higher practical limits:** Bank transfers are normally better suited to larger deposits than debit or credit cards.
- **Clearer records:** Transfers from a personal bank account create a straightforward funding trail.
- **Useful for recurring purchases:** A connected bank account can be more convenient for regular buying than entering card details repeatedly.
- **Trading access before full settlement:** OKX says crypto purchased through ACH may be available for trading immediately, even though withdrawals can remain restricted during a short holding period.

There are also drawbacks:

- ACH may not be available in every state.
- Your OKX account name must match the name on the linked bank account.
- The crypto may not be immediately transferable to an external wallet.
- A bank transfer is not the same thing as an instant, fully settled cash deposit.
- Your bank may reject or delay a transfer involving a crypto platform.

For a small, urgent purchase, a card or another payment method may feel faster. For a larger purchase or a regular buying schedule, ACH is often the more cost-conscious route.

## ACH versus wire transfer

“Bank transfer” can refer to several different payment rails. In the U.S., ACH and wire transfers are the two most important ones to understand.

| Feature | ACH transfer | Wire transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical user | Retail buyers and recurring investors | Larger deposits or users who need faster settlement |
| Speed | Often slower to settle fully | Often same business day, depending on bank and cutoff time |
| Cost | Often free for the customer on the exchange side | Your bank may charge a wire fee |
| Setup | Usually linked through a bank-connection service such as Plaid | Requires exact wire instructions and a reference |
| Withdrawal restrictions | A temporary hold may apply | Depends on the transfer and account history |
| Best use | Routine purchases and dollar-cost averaging | Larger or time-sensitive deposits |

OKX’s own educational material describes ACH as cost-effective but potentially slower, with settlement commonly taking several business days. It describes wires as faster but notes that banks may charge fees to send them.

The exact timing is not guaranteed. Weekends, bank holidays, account verification, transfer reviews, and incorrect payment details can all add delays. “Instant” usually means that the platform lets you place a trade before the banking rail has fully settled, not that the entire banking process has completed in seconds.

## What does it cost to buy crypto by bank transfer?

There are several different costs that can appear in the transaction. Calling the purchase “free” without separating them is how people end up comparing apples to blockchain transactions.

### ACH payment fee

OKX states that ACH purchases generally have no fee, but it also tells users to review the transaction summary for the final details.

That means “no ACH fee” does not necessarily mean the market price, spread, and trading fee are irrelevant. Always check the preview before approving the order.

### Trading fee

If you deposit dollars and then use the spot trading interface, the applicable trading fee depends on your account tier and the trading pair. OKX’s current U.S. framework divides spot pairs into fee groups.

For regular U.S. users, the published maker fee is **0.200%** and the taker fee is **0.350%** across the listed groups. A maker order adds liquidity to the order book; a taker order executes against existing liquidity.

The difference is small on a single purchase but becomes more meaningful for frequent trading. On a $1,000 taker trade at 0.350%, the trading fee would be $3.50 before considering any other pricing effects. The exact amount shown by OKX at order confirmation should take priority over a general example.

### Spread and quoted purchase price

A direct buy flow can display a quoted price rather than the same interface used for an order-book trade. The final amount of crypto you receive may reflect the quoted price, spread, and any applicable transaction fee.

For that reason, compare the final crypto amount rather than looking only for a line labeled “fee.” A transaction can show a low explicit fee while still using a less favorable quote than a limit order on the spot market.

### Bank wire charges

If you use a wire transfer instead of ACH, your bank may charge its own outgoing-wire fee. OKX’s educational guide notes that bank wire fees commonly come from the sending bank, although the amount depends on the institution.

A rough comparison looks like this:

### ACH

Best for regular purchases, larger deposits, and users who do not need immediate withdrawal access.

- Usually low-cost or free on the payment side.
- Can provide trading access before full settlement.
- May include a withdrawal hold.
- Available only to eligible verified users.

### Wire transfer

Best for users who need to move a larger amount quickly and are comfortable checking detailed bank instructions.

- Can settle faster than ACH.
- May incur an outgoing fee from your bank.

### Debit or credit card

Best for speed or convenience when the purchase is relatively small.

- Often processes quickly.
- May include a payment-processing fee.
- May have lower limits.
- Can be more expensive for repeated buying.

### Existing cash balance

If your OKX account already has cleared cash, buying from that balance can avoid repeating the bank-funding step. OKX says users can buy crypto with a cash balance, subject to the applicable transaction details and account conditions.
